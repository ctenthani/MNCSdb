# MNCS Registry V1.0 — association ownership and awards

The association owns daily sport administration. Saving an athlete, team, fixture
or result finishes that action; no council approval queue is involved. Competitions
still require MNCS acceptance. Council receives reports, nomination dossiers,
funding requests, travel requests, MRA clearance and other repeatable private cases.

## A simpler workspace

- Persistent signed-in name, email, role and association; a My workspace shortcut.
- Plain-language navigation, collapsed entry forms and sport/category defaults.
- Competition-specific participant choices; selecting an athlete clears the team
  choice and vice versa. Position is requested only for placed results.
- Request forms show only fields relevant to the selected request.
- Clickable awards cards preselect category, including junior birth-date checks.
- Separate workspaces for judges, auditors and fans.

Existing routine records retain their history. Saving an old editable record adopts
association ownership and updates its public projection. Internally the legacy
Approved status represents a saved routine record; the interface labels it Saved.
Publication requires public consent for athlete names. DOB/evidence remain private.
Published projections identify routine data as Association-reported. Rankings do
not claim independent MNCS verification of those performances.

## Rankings

Associations or MNCS admins create immutable placing-points rules. One best placing
per competition contributes, then the best N competition scores are summed.
Equal totals share rank. Compare within the same discipline, category, season and
rule; these leaderboards are not cross-sport rankings or awards decisions.

## Official awards and separate fan participation

All 14 handbook categories remain available. MNCS screens dossiers and chooses up
to three consented finalists per category. Personality finalists must be certified
winners from individual athlete categories. Junior eligibility is strictly under 20
on the configured date; that date locks once nominations have been submitted.

MNCS must approve numerical criterion weights because the handbook supplies
qualitative criteria, not a universal points formula. Suggested form weights are
editable defaults, not handbook-prescribed weights. Judging locks the approved
rubric and official assignments. Default judge count is five; MNCS can configure
1–7 and must use an approved operational quorum. Conflicts must be resolved before
judging: ballots require a no-conflict declaration and reject matching recorded
association affiliations. A judge cannot replace a submitted ballot.

Criterion score = mark (0–10) × criterion weight / 10. A judge's total is out of
100. The official score is the average of every assigned judge's completed ballot.
Equal totals receive joint ranks/winners; alphabetical display ordering does not
break ties. MNCS approves this tie rule with the rubric.

The handbook's original 70% judge / 30% public policy is replaced only after MNCS
records approval and public disclosure of judges-only scoring. Fan polls explicitly
have zero official weight. One verified email may vote once per category, enforced
by both account and normalized-email uniqueness. This prevents repeat votes from
the same email; it cannot prove one person has only one email. Fan totals are hidden
until official results are published and the poll is closed.

An assigned auditor can read sealed ballots only after judging closes. Certification
stores an immutable criteria/results/ballots snapshot and SHA-256 fingerprint;
publication requires certification and a closed fan poll. The fingerprint detects
changes to that snapshot. It is not a substitute for an independent human audit or
an external auditor's professional opinion. Database owners remain privileged;
export the confidential audit to the auditor's controlled archive for assurance.

Judges can read their assigned nomination dossiers/PDFs, not unrelated cases or
other judges' ballots. Public output never includes judge identities, private
ballots, voter emails or confidential supporting documents.

## Tournament planner

Round robin supports 2–32 teams, including odd-team byes. Knockout supports 2, 4,
8, 16 or 32 teams with a full bracket. Teams must belong to the accepted competition's
association, sport and category. Seed order is the displayed team order. Fixtures
are scheduled sequentially at one venue, in Malawi time, within competition dates.
Set a suitable interval for play and rest; the planner does not optimize travel,
multiple venues or television schedules. Existing fixtures block a second draw.

A saved winning team result is required before knockout progression. The next
round's fixture is created when both entrants are known. Duplicate fixture winners
are rejected. Results that already advanced a bracket cannot change the winner;
generated fixtures can be postponed/cancelled but cannot be archived independently.
Bracket regeneration, rollback of advanced rounds, knockout byes, double round
robin and automatic league standings are not included in this release.

## Deployment and operation

Use START_HERE.md. The complete transactional INSTALL_ALL.sql upgrades existing
accounts and records; backup first. New judge/auditor accounts use the existing
Netlify account service. No new private environment variables are required.
Verified public polls additionally need Supabase email signups, an OTP template,
SMTP and the correct redirect configuration. Browser testing after deployment
must confirm real Auth emails, storage PDFs, Functions provisioning and RLS.

The app provides nomination screening and voting workflows, not automatic
handbook compliance: committee appointments, conflict resolution, independent
audit engagement and time-limited appeals need MNCS procedures. Other request
can receive an appeal; automatic appeal deadlines/adjudication are not implemented.
No production data or deployed site was modified during this release.
